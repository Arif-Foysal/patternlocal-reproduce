
\abstract{Suppressor variables, input features that carry no information about the target yet correlate with the structured noise surrounding informative features, are a persistent failure mode of local explanation methods, which routinely assign them false-positive attributions. PatternLocal mitigates this by converting discriminative saliency into generative signal patterns, but relies on randomized perturbation sampling, per-sample surrogate fitting, and coarse superpixel groupings that limit spatial detail. We introduce PatternGrad, a deterministic local explainer that couples pixel-wise Integrated Gradients with data-adaptive pattern conversion: the gradient surrogate is transformed by the covariance of its $k$ nearest training neighbors (kernel-weighted, $k \ll D$), yielding exact single-pass explanations with no perturbation sampling, no surrogate optimization, and pixel-level detail inaccessible to superpixels. On the XAI-TRIS benchmark, which spans linear, XOR, and rigid-transformation tasks under white and correlated background noise across 47 evaluation settings, PatternGrad ranks first or second of 13 methods on false-positive attribution (Importance Mass Error, IME) in the majority of settings, surpasses every classical gradient and perturbation baseline on both attribution metrics, and matches or exceeds PatternLocal on IME at nearly every setting. Under rigid geometric transformations it is the only non-supervised-filter method that holds a top-two localization score (Earth Mover's Distance, EMD), while an ablated variant without path integration collapses to rank 13, isolating the source of this robustness. PatternGrad explains in 0.010--0.074\,s per instance, a 9--79$\times$ speedup over PatternLocal driven by a single fit-once neighborhood index and a small-$k$ covariance solve, establishing a state-of-the-art trade-off between attribution fidelity and runtime.}
