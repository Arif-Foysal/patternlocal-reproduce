# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper introduces PatternGrad, a deterministic local explanation method designed to mitigate false-positive feature attributions caused by correlated background noise (suppressor variables) in non-linear machine learning models. Building upon the local generative pattern-conversion framework of PatternLocal, PatternGrad replaces stochastic perturbation-based surrogates (such as LIME) with the deterministic, path-integrated attribution vector of Integrated Gradients (IG) evaluated directly in native pixel space. By projecting this analytical surrogate response through a kernel-weighted local covariance matrix over the $K$ nearest training neighbors via a closed-form, matrix-free two-pass operator of $\mathcal{O}(KD)$ complexity, the method avoids per-instance Monte Carlo mask sampling and surrogate regression. Empirical evaluations across 47 accuracy-gated configurations of the synthetic XAI-TRIS benchmark (spanning linear, XOR, and rigid geometric transformation tasks under white and correlated noise) demonstrate that PatternGrad effectively suppresses off-support background attributions and improves spatial localization under rigid transformations while substantially accelerating the covariance conversion stage.

## Strengths
* The paper addresses a well-established and practically important failure mode in post-hoc explainable AI—the tendency of discriminative attribution methods to assign false-positive importance to correlated suppressor variables—which is particularly relevant to domains with correlated sensor or physiological background variation.
* Instantiating local covariance pattern conversion with a deterministic, path-integrated, instance-scaled gradient surrogate eliminates Monte Carlo perturbation sampling variance and resolves the local gradient saturation that causes raw pointwise gradient conversion to collapse under rigid geometric transformations.
* The formulation of a closed-form, matrix-free two-pass matrix–vector product ($\mathcal{O}(KD)$ time and space complexity) provides a clean, reproducible computational procedure that avoids explicitly materializing $D \times D$ local covariance matrices, backed by thorough empirical and statistical comparisons across the XAI-TRIS SNR sweeps.

## Weaknesses
* The core mathematical formulation—regressing centered local neighborhood features onto a scalar local surrogate response using an adaptive median-bandwidth Gaussian kernel over $K$ nearest training neighbors and deriving the closed-form $\ell_2$-regularized covariance ratio—was already established by PatternLocal, which explicitly noted that its framework accommodates gradient-based local surrogates alongside LIME and KernelSHAP (Reference Paper: Minimizing False-Positive Attributions in Explanations of Non-Linear Models, Anders Gjølbye, Stefan Haufe, Lars Kai Hansen, 2025).
* There is a theoretical tension between Proposition 1, which proves exact suppressor cancellation under the premise that the surrogate response acts as a pure denoised signal estimate, and PatternGrad's instance-scaled surrogate $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \odot \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ (accompanied by an algebraic misstatement of the Completeness identity on page 8); as shown in Remark 1, instance scaling breaks exact noise cancellation even on the canonical linear suppressor model, and the ablation study (`PG-Grad`, $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}_\star)$) alters both path integration and instance scaling simultaneously without evaluating the unscaled path-integrated slope $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ to isolate the two design axes.
* In Section 4.3, coupling the lower bound of PatternGrad's neighborhood search grid ($k_{\text{floor}}$) to PatternLocal's tuned locality ratio ($k_{\text{ratio}}$) creates an asymmetric hyperparameter comparison: whenever PatternLocal selects $k_{\text{ratio}} = 1.0$, PatternGrad's search grid collapses to a single global candidate ($K = 36,000$), forcing global covariance pooling across uncorrelated polarity sub-clusters on `XOR` tasks and contributing to PatternGrad's elevated Earth Mover's Distance at high SNR.
* The reported $0.007\text{--}0.173\text{ s}$ per-instance runtimes and $3.4\times\text{--}82\times$ speedups measure only the $\mathcal{O}(KD)$ covariance conversion stage—excluding both the surrogate computation front-ends ($M = 50$ IG backward passes vs. $N_{\mathcal{L}} = 2,000$ LIME forward passes and ridge regression) and the $\mathcal{O}(ND)$ nearest-neighbor retrieval—which reflects software implementation differences over the reference PatternLocal codebase rather than the algorithmic speedup of bypassing perturbation sampling; please consider reporting full end-to-end wall-clock timings.
* Although overcoming superpixel discretization artifacts is framed as one of the three primary motivations and contributions over LIME-driven PatternLocal, all experiments actually instantiate LIME and PatternLocal at native pixel resolution ($D' = D = 4,096$ without superpixels) in an under-determined regression regime ($N_{\mathcal{L}} = 2,000 < D'$), and the empirical validation is restricted to synthetic $64 \times 64$ XAI-TRIS images without evaluating deterministic neural pattern baselines or real-world physiological datasets (Reference Paper: Learning how to explain neural networks: PatternNet and PatternAttribution, Pieter-Jan Kindermans, Kristof T. Schütt, Maximilian Alber, Klaus-Robert Müller, Dumitru Erhan, Been Kim, Sven Dähne, 2018; Reference Paper: Minimizing False-Positive Attributions in Explanations of Non-Linear Models, Anders Gjølbye, Stefan Haufe, Lars Kai Hansen, 2025).

### [PatternGrad Formulation, Theoretical Guarantees, and Algorithm] (Pages: 6-12)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

* **Verification Summary:** The derivation of the centered kernel-weighted ridge regression estimator (Eqs. 7–9), the algebraic distinction between the centered and uncentered normal equations, the $\mathcal{O}(KD)$ matrix-free two-pass evaluation in Algorithm 1, the asymptotic limit analyses in Proposition 2 and Remark 2, and the overall per-instance computational complexity accounting $\mathcal{O}(ND + MC_{\text{back}} + KD)$ were verified and found to be mathematically sound. However, there are a few mathematical and conceptual points regarding the completeness identity, coordinate scaling, and the applicability of Proposition 1 to the instance-scaled surrogate that warrant attention.

1. **Incorrect Application of the *Completeness* Axiom and Coordinate-Scaling Mismatch in the Surrogate Response $\tilde{y}^{(j)}$ (Section 3.1.1, pp. 7–8)**
   - **Definitive Mathematical Error in the Completeness Identity:** On page 8 (lines 11–14), the use of the instance-scaled Integrated Gradients vector $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = \hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star) = (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \odot \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ as the linear weight vector in $\tilde{y}^{(j)} = (\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}})^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}$ is justified by stating:
     > *"and by Completeness the response satisfies $\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) = f(\mathbf{x}_\star) - f(\mathbf{r})$, keeping $\tilde{y}$ commensurate with the model-output contrast between target and baseline."*
     
     Because $\hat{s}_i^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star) = (x_{\star,i} - r_i)\bar{g}_i(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ already incorporates the element-wise displacement $(x_{\star,i} - r_i)$ (Eq. 2), the *Completeness* axiom (as stated in Section 2.1, page 4, up to $M$-step Riemann quadrature error) is
     $$\mathbf{1}^\top \hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star) = \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \approx f(\mathbf{x}_\star) - f(\mathbf{r}).$$
     Taking the inner product of $\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ with $(\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r})$ multiplies by the displacement a second time, yielding a quadratically displacement-weighted sum:
     $$\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^D (x_{\star,i} - r_i)^2 \bar{g}_i(\mathbf{x}_\star) \neq f(\mathbf{x}_\star) - f(\mathbf{r}).$$
   - **Potential Conceptual Concern Regarding Coordinate Rescaling and Dimensional Units:** In the preceding sentences on page 8 (lines 3–7), the text notes that $w_i^{\text{PG}} = (x_{\star,i} - r_i)\bar{g}_i$ are the slopes of $f$ with respect to the dimensionless instance-normalized coordinates $u_i = x_i / (x_{\star,i} - r_i)$. However, a first-order linear expansion in the $u_i$ coordinate system requires multiplying the slope $\partial f / \partial u_i = w_i^{\text{PG}}$ by the centered *normalized* neighbor displacement $\tilde{u}_i^{(j)} = \tilde{x}_i^{(j)} / (x_{\star,i} - r_i)$, in which case the $(x_{\star,i} - r_i)$ factors cancel and reduce to the unscaled slope projection $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}$. Because Section 3.1.2 (Eq. 8) and Algorithm 1 (Line 5) instead take the inner product of $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ with the *unnormalized* neighbor displacement $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}$,
     $$\tilde{y}^{(j)} = (\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}})^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)} = \sum_{i=1}^D \bar{g}_i(\mathbf{x}_\star)\,(x_{\star,i} - r_i)\,\tilde{x}_i^{(j)},$$
     each coordinate is weighted by the product of two displacements, $(x_{\star,i} - r_i)\tilde{x}_i^{(j)}$. Consequently, $\tilde{y}^{(j)}$ carries units of $[f] \cdot [x_i]$ rather than units of the model output $[f]$, and scales quadratically with coordinate rescaling. Moreover, whenever a target feature has negative displacement ($x_{\star,i} - r_i < 0$), $w_i^{\text{PG}}$ flips the sign of the local sensitivity $\bar{g}_i$ when projecting $\tilde{x}_i^{(j)}$, which can cause opposite-polarity features within a multi-part signal (such as in the `XOR` task) to partially cancel in the scalar response $\tilde{y}^{(j)}$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Correct the Completeness expression on page 8 to $\mathbf{1}^\top \hat{\mathbf{s}}^{\text{IG}}(\mathbf{x}_\star) = \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \approx f(\mathbf{x}_\star) - f(\mathbf{r})$, and revise the coordinate-transformation discussion to clarify that projecting unnormalized neighbor displacements $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}$ onto $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ acts as a diagonal instance-displacement weighting $\tilde{y}^{(j)} = \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r})\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}$ on top of the local slope rather than a unit-consistent first-order linear expansion in $u_i$.

2. **Tension Between the Denoising Premise of Proposition 1 and PatternGrad's Instance-Scaled Surrogate $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ (Section 3.1.3, Proposition 1 and Remark 1, pp. 9–11)**
   - **Potential Theoretical Concern:** Proposition 1 proves exact suppressor cancellation ($P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$) under the premise that the centered surrogate response $\tilde{y} = (\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}})^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ decomposes as $\tilde{y} = \psi(\mathbf{s}) + \varepsilon$ with $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}, \varepsilon] = \mathbf{0}$. Because $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[\mathbf{s}, P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}] = \mathbf{0}$ is also assumed by hypothesis, these two premises directly imply $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}, \tilde{y}] = \mathbf{0}$ (the numerator of $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{a}$ in Eq. 9) by the bilinearity of covariance for any scalar variable $\tilde{y}$, without invoking the definition of $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ in Eq. (6). More importantly, to substantiate that this denoising premise holds in practice, the first remark following the proof (page 10, lines 12–18) states:
     > *"First, the denoising property of $\tilde{y}$ is exactly what the suppressor weights $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ achieve on canonical linear models: on the two-variable suppressor model of Remark 1, the Bayes-optimal filter $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (1, -1)^\top$ yields $\tilde{y} = \boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf{x} = S$, a pure signal estimate with $\varepsilon = 0$, precisely because the non-zero suppressor weight cancels the noise $N$ shared by both coordinates; the balance $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{C}_K P_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} + P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{C}_K P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = \mathbf{0}$ is the population form of this cancellation."*
     
     This explanation conflates the unscaled linear filter $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ with PatternGrad's instance-scaled surrogate $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \odot \boldsymbol{\beta}$. As Remark 1 subsequently demonstrates on pages 10–11, on that exact two-variable linear model ($X_1 = S + N$, $X_2 = N$, $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (1, -1)^\top$) evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_\star = (s_\star + n_\star, n_\star)^\top$ with $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$, the pure signal estimate $\tilde{y} = \boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{S}$ ($\varepsilon = 0$) holds only for the *unscaled* slope $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star) = \boldsymbol{\beta}$ (corresponding to the PG-Grad ablation in linear models). PatternGrad's actual instance-scaled surrogate is $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = (s_\star + n_\star, -n_\star)^\top$, which yields the centered response
     $$\tilde{y} = (\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}})^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = (s_\star + n_\star)(\tilde{S} + \tilde{N}) - n_\star \tilde{N} = \underbrace{(s_\star + n_\star)\tilde{S}}_{\psi(\mathbf{s})} + \underbrace{s_\star \tilde{N}}_{\varepsilon}.$$
     Here, the noise residual $\varepsilon = s_\star \tilde{N}$ violates the premise $\operatorname{Cov}[P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}, \varepsilon] = \mathbf{0}$ whenever the target instance carries non-zero signal $s_\star \neq 0$, since $\operatorname{Cov}(X_2, \varepsilon) = s_\star \operatorname{Var}(N) = s_\star \neq 0$. Thus, pre-multiplying a denoising filter by non-uniform target displacements $(x_{\star,i} - r_i)$ breaks the noise-cancellation balance and violates the premise of Proposition 1 even on the canonical linear suppressor model.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify the scope of Proposition 1 and the post-proof commentary on page 10 so that they do not conflate the unscaled slope response $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ with the instance-scaled response $(\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}})^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}}$. For example, either state Proposition 1 for a general surrogate weight vector $\mathbf{w}$ (explicitly noting that the unscaled slope $\mathbf{w} = \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ satisfies the denoising condition exactly in the canonical linear regime while $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$ satisfies it when the active displacement scaling is uniform across coupled coordinates), or state the denoising premise in terms of $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)^\top \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \psi(\mathbf{s}) + \varepsilon$ and explicitly express the residual noise-subspace attribution $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{a} = c^{-1}P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{C}_K\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r})\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ characterized in Remark 1.

3. **Conflation of Finite-$K$ Empirical Covariance and Population Covariance Notation in Proposition 1 (Sections 3.1.2–3.1.3, pp. 8–10)**
   - **Potential Clarity Concern:** In Section 3.1.2 (page 8, lines 28–29), $\mathbf{C}_K := \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{x}_\star)} \pi_j \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)\top}$, $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[\mathbf{x}, \tilde{y}] = \mathbf{C}_K \mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$, and $\mathbf{a}$ (Eq. 9) are defined as finite-$K$ empirical sample quantities over the retrieved training neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{x}_\star) \subset \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$. Proposition 1 and its proof (pages 9–10) use these exact same finite-sample symbols ($\mathbf{C}_K$, $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}$, and $\mathbf{a}$) to state exact orthogonality conditions ($\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[\mathbf{s}, P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}] = \mathbf{0}$ and $\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_{\mathbf{x}_\star}^K}[P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{x}, \varepsilon] = \mathbf{0}$) and conclude $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$, before the second remark following the proof (page 10, lines 18–23) clarifies that *"the cancellation is stated at the population level of the kernel-weighted neighborhood: at finite $K$, $\mathbf{C}_K$ is an empirical covariance estimated from $K$ neighbors, so the balance holds only up to sampling fluctuation of order $\mathcal{O}_p(K^{-1/2})$."* Using the same symbols for both the finite-$K$ empirical estimator in Eq. (9) and the population operator in Proposition 1 creates a notation conflict.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Distinguish the population locally weighted covariance operator from the finite-$K$ empirical sample covariance $\mathbf{C}_K$ in Proposition 1, or state explicitly within Proposition 1 that under the population orthogonality premises, the finite-$K$ estimator satisfies $P_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{a} = \mathcal{O}_p(K^{-1/2})$.

### [Experimental Protocol, Hyperparameter Selection, and Evaluation Metrics] (Pages: 13-19)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Asymmetric Hyperparameter Search Grids, Cross-Method Floor Coupling, and Reported Coincidence Counts (Section 4.3, p. 17; Table 1, p. 18; Section 5.1 & Table 2, pp. 19–20; Section 5.5, p. 25; Section 5.9, p. 30):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section 4.3, PatternLocal's neighborhood ratio is tuned over a three-point grid $k_{\text{ratio}} \in \{0.05, 0.5, 1.0\}$ (corresponding to $K \in \{1{,}800, 18{,}000, 36{,}000\}$), whereas PatternGrad's neighborhood size $K$ is tuned over a candidate grid coupled to PatternLocal's selected value on the same 30-instance tuning subset:
     $$\mathcal{K} = \{k_{\text{floor}}, 5{,}000, 10{,}000, 20{,}000, N_{\text{train}}\} \cap [k_{\text{floor}}, N_{\text{train}}], \quad k_{\text{floor}} = \max(1, \lfloor k_{\text{ratio}} N_{\text{train}} \rfloor).$$
     This cross-method floor coupling introduces two structural asymmetries and a reporting discrepancy in the baseline comparison:
     1. *Grid granularity advantage at small $k_{\text{ratio}}$:* When PatternLocal selects $k_{\text{ratio}} = 0.05$ ($k_{\text{floor}} = 1{,}800$), PatternGrad searches over five candidate neighborhood sizes ($\{1{,}800, 5{,}000, 10{,}000, 20{,}000, 36{,}000\}$), offering it intermediate neighborhood scales ($5{,}000, 10{,}000, 20{,}000$) that are never made available to PatternLocal (selected in four configurations: `LIN-CORR` at $\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.4\}$ and `RIGID-CORR` at $\alpha \in \{0.2, 0.4\}$ in Table 2). Similarly, when PatternLocal selects $k_{\text{ratio}} = 0.5$ ($k_{\text{floor}} = 18{,}000$), the intermediate candidate $K = 20{,}000$ remains available exclusively to PatternGrad.
     2. *Degenerate grid collapse at $k_{\text{ratio}} = 1.0$ and forced global neighborhood on `XOR`:* Conversely, whenever PatternLocal selects $k_{\text{ratio}} = 1.0$ ($k_{\text{floor}} = 36{,}000$, which occurs in 28 of the 47 configurations, including all `LIN-WHITE`, all `XOR-WHITE`, and `XOR-CORR` at $\alpha \ge 0.4$), PatternGrad's candidate set collapses to the singleton $\mathcal{K} = \{36{,}000\}$, preventing PatternGrad from evaluating any local neighborhood ($K < 36{,}000$). In Section 5.5 (p. 25) and Section 5.9 (Finding 5, p. 30), the authors explain that PatternGrad's elevated EMD on `XOR` at high SNR ($0.123$ on `XOR-WHITE` and $0.095$ on `XOR-CORR` at $\alpha = 0.9$, compared to $0.040$ and $0.033$ for PatternLocal) is caused by pooling across all four sign-alternating polarity sub-clusters ($\pm\mathbf{a}^{\text{T}} \pm \mathbf{a}^{\text{L}}$) under the global neighborhood $K = 36{,}000$, where the two disjoint tetrominoes are globally uncorrelated in $\mathbf{C}_K$ and thus cannot rebalance intra-support mass asymmetries in $\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}}$.      3. *Discrepancy in Reported Coincidence Counts (Section 5.1, p. 20):* On page 20 (lines 9–14), the text states that PatternGrad's selected $K$ coincided with PatternLocal's floor $k_{\text{floor}}$ in 39 of the 47 configurations and exceeded it in the remaining 8, while simultaneously stating that PatternLocal selected $k_{\text{ratio}} = 0.05$ ($K = 1{,}800$) in 16 configurations, $0.5$ ($K = 18{,}000$) in 3, and $1.0$ ($K = 36{,}000$) in 28. This implies PatternGrad exceeded PatternLocal's floor in at least $5 + 4 = 9$ configurations (and coincided in at most $47 - 9 = 38$), rather than 8 and 39.
   - **Unspecified Tuning Protocol for the `PG-Grad` Ablation:** Sections 4.2.2 and 4.3 specify how hyperparameters are tuned for LIME ($\sigma_L$), PatternLocal ($k_{\text{ratio}}$), and PatternGrad ($K$), but do not state whether the `PG-Grad` (`analytical_grad`) ablation has its neighborhood size $K$ tuned independently on the 30-instance tuning subset or reuses the $K$ selected for PatternGrad (`analytical_ig`).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** To ensure strict parity and evaluate each estimator's intrinsic sensitivity to $K$, decouple the searches and tune PatternGrad, `PG-Grad`, and PatternLocal independently over the exact same candidate set of neighborhood sizes (e.g., $K \in \{1{,}800, 5{,}000, 10{,}000, 18{,}000, 20{,}000, 36{,}000\}$)—or at minimum report an uncoupled ablation on `XOR` testing whether allowing local $K$ (such as $K = 1{,}800$) resolves the high-SNR `XOR` mass imbalance—explicitly state the tuning protocol used for `PG-Grad` in Section 4.3, and reconcile the coincidence/exceedance counts (38 vs. 39 and 9 vs. 8) with the entries in Table 2.

2. **Discrepancy Between the Superpixel Motivation and the Pixel-Level LIME Instantiation, and Under-Sampled Surrogate Regression (Abstract, p. 1; Section 1, pp. 3–4; Section 2.2, p. 5; Section 3.1, p. 6; Section 4.2.2, pp. 15–17; Table 1, p. 18):**
   - **Definitive Inconsistency in Motivation and Baseline Description:** In the Abstract (p. 1), Section 1 (pp. 3–4), Section 2.2 (p. 5), and Section 3.1 (p. 6), one of the three primary limitations attributed to LIME-driven PatternLocal [22]—and one of the three main contributions claimed for PatternGrad—is overcoming the coarse spatial resolution and boundary artifacts caused by superpixel discretization ($D' < D$).    - **Potential Concern (Under-Sampled Pixel-Level Regression):** Fitting a $D' = 4{,}096$-dimensional linear surrogate using only $N_{\mathcal{L}} = 2{,}000$ random binary mask samples per instance places pixel-level LIME in an under-determined regime ($N_{\mathcal{L}} < D'$), where the regression relies heavily on the ridge penalty ($\lambda_L = 1$) via the Woodbury solver and incurs elevated Monte Carlo estimation variance rather than superpixel coarseness.    - **Potential Concern:** Section 5.5 compares PatternGrad ($\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \odot \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$) against a single ablation variant, `PG-Grad` ($\mathbf{w}^{\text{PG}} = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}_\star)$).    - **Impact on Validity and Interpretation:** While $\bar{\mathbf{g}} \approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f$ approximately holds in the near-linear `LIN` configurations, the two axes are strongly confounded in the non-linear `RIGID` and `XOR` regimes:
     1. In `RIGID`, the active tetromino occupies only a small fraction of the $64 \times 64$ image while background pixels have smaller magnitudes or zero baseline-relative displacement. Multiplying by $(\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r})$ (with $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$) acts as an instance-specific spatial gate that attenuates diffuse background sensitivities where $x_{\star,i} \approx 0$ before covariance projection, whereas the unscaled gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ retains non-zero sensitivity across all possible translated/rotated tetromino locations in the MLP's receptive field.    - **Suggested Improvement:** Because the path-averaged gradient $\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ is already computed during Step 1 of Algorithm 1 prior to element-wise multiplication by $(\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r})$, evaluating the unscaled path-integrated surrogate $\mathbf{w} = \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ (and/or the instance-scaled pointwise gradient $\mathbf{w} = (\mathbf{x}_\star - \mathbf{r}) \odot \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}_\star)$, i.e., $\text{Input} \times \text{Gradient}$) in Table 3 requires zero additional backward passes and would cleanly orthogonalize the $2 \times 2$ design space across `LIN`, `XOR`, and `RIGID`.
